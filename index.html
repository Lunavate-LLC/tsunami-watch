    How it works

    In seats Trump carried comfortably, a close race is a wave sign and a Democratic win is a tsunami sign, following the early-race guides from NPR and Daily Kos.

    In competitive races, the wave line is the analysts' marker or the final poll, and the tsunami line sits 6 points beyond it, about the size of Marquette's biggest final-poll miss since 2016. These tsunami lines are our own working lines, not a forecaster's.

    Tonight's pace weights each race by the share of its vote counted. A race still counting is filled out at that pace for the rest of its vote, and a race with no results yet is projected at its wave line plus that pace. The projected statuses give the heading, by the same rules as the verdict: half or more tsunami signs is tsunami pace, half or more tsunami or wave signs is wave pace.

    The heading is an early sign until 3 races' worth of votes are counted (two races each half counted make one). It's confirmed once half the bellwethers are called and the called races alone give the same verdict.

    Senate seats count Democrats not up this year, races called for them and races they lead. The projection adds races without results at tonight's pace. An independent who wins is shown separately and not counted toward 53.
